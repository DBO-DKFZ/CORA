# Superficial fungal infections — demo textbook chapter (SYNTHETIC)

This chapter is synthetic text written solely to exercise the CORA pipeline in the
demo. It is **not** a medical textbook and must not be used for patient care.

## Tinea corporis

Tinea corporis presents as an annular plaque with a raised, scaly, advancing border
and relative central clearing. Diagnosis is confirmed by potassium hydroxide
microscopy of scale taken from the active edge, or by culture.

Limited disease is treated with a topical allylamine such as terbinafine cream twice
daily for two weeks. Extensive disease, or disease that has failed topical therapy,
is treated with oral terbinafine.

## Tinea capitis

Tinea capitis requires **oral** antifungal therapy; topical agents do not penetrate
the hair follicle and are used only as adjunctive sporicidal shampoo to reduce
transmission. Griseofulvin remains the reference agent for *Microsporum* infection,
whereas terbinafine is preferred for *Trichophyton* infection in this demo chapter.

## Onychomycosis

Confirm the diagnosis mycologically before starting systemic therapy, because roughly
half of dystrophic nails in this demo chapter have a non-fungal cause such as
psoriasis or trauma. Oral terbinafine is the first-line systemic agent for
dermatophyte onychomycosis.
