# Acne vulgaris — demo guideline (SYNTHETIC)

This document is synthetic text written solely to exercise the CORA pipeline in the
demo. It is **not** a clinical guideline and must not be used for patient care.

## Definition and classification

Acne vulgaris is a chronic inflammatory disorder of the pilosebaceous unit. For the
purposes of this demo document, severity is graded as:

- **Comedonal acne**: open and closed comedones only, no inflammatory lesions.
- **Mild to moderate papulopustular acne**: comedones plus inflammatory papules and
  pustules, without nodules.
- **Severe papulopustular or nodular acne**: numerous inflammatory lesions, nodules,
  or scarring.

## Topical treatment of mild to moderate papulopustular acne

For mild to moderate papulopustular acne, the recommended first-line treatment in
this demo document is a **topical fixed-dose combination of adapalene and benzoyl
peroxide**, applied once daily in the evening. The combination targets both
comedogenesis and inflammatory lesions, and benzoyl peroxide limits the emergence of
antibiotic-resistant *Cutibacterium acnes*.

Topical antibiotic monotherapy is **not** recommended at any severity because of
resistance selection. If a topical antibiotic is used, it must always be combined
with benzoyl peroxide.

## Systemic treatment

Systemic treatment is reserved for severe papulopustular, nodular, or scarring acne,
or for moderate disease that has failed an adequate topical trial of at least
12 weeks.

- **Oral doxycycline** 100 mg once daily, always combined with a topical retinoid and
  benzoyl peroxide, and limited to a maximum of 12 weeks.
- **Oral isotretinoin** for severe nodular or scarring acne, or for relapsing disease.
  Pregnancy prevention is mandatory for patients who can become pregnant.

## Monitoring

Response is assessed after 8 to 12 weeks. Maintenance with a topical retinoid is
recommended after clearance to prevent relapse.
