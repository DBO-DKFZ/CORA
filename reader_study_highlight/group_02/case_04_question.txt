A <strong>16-year-old girl</strong> is brought to the physician for evaluation of <strong>severe acne</strong> on her <strong>face, chest, and back</strong> for the past <strong>2 years</strong>. She has <strong>no itching or scaling</strong> associated with the lesions. She has been treated in the past with a combination of <strong>oral cephalexin</strong> and <strong>topical benzoyl peroxide</strong> without <strong>clinical improvement</strong>. She is <strong>sexually active</strong> with <strong>1 male partner</strong>, and they use <strong>condoms inconsistently</strong>. She does not smoke, drink alcohol, or use illicit drugs. There is no personal or family history of serious illness. Her vital signs are within normal limits. Examination shows <strong>mild facial scarring</strong> and numerous <strong>open comedones</strong> and <strong>sebaceous skin lesions</strong> on her <strong>face, chest, and back</strong>. Which of the following is indicated prior to initiating the <strong>next most appropriate step in treatment</strong>?

A. Administer oral contraceptives
B. Measure creatinine kinase levels
C. Measure serum beta-hCG levels
D. Screen for depression with a questionnaire
E. Switch cephalexin to doxycycline

LLM answer: C. Measure serum beta-hCG levels
