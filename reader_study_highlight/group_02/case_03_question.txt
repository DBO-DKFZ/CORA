A <strong>23-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician because of <strong>lightening of his skin</strong> on <strong>both hands</strong>, <strong>under his eyes</strong>, and on his <strong>neck</strong> for <strong>2 years</strong>. During this period the lesions <strong>slowly grew in size</strong>. There is <strong>no itchiness or pain</strong>. He regularly visits his family in <strong>India</strong>. <strong>Vital signs are within normal limits</strong>. Examination shows <strong>sharply demarcated, depigmented skin patches</strong> on the <strong>dorsum of both hands</strong>, in the <strong>periocular region</strong>, and on the <strong>neck</strong>. <strong>Sensation of the skin is intact</strong>. The lesions <strong>fluoresce blue-white under Wood's lamp</strong>. Which of the following findings is most likely to be associated with this patient's diagnosis?

A. “Spaghetti and meatballs” appearance on KOH scraping
B. Decreased pigment with normal melanocyte count
C. Elevated anti-TPO antibody levels
D. Renal angiomyolipoma on abdominal MRI

LLM answer: C. Elevated anti-TPO antibody levels
