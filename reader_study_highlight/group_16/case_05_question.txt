A <strong>19-year-old woman</strong> with a known history of <strong>malabsorption</strong> presents with a <strong>painful red tongue</strong>, <strong>red eyes</strong>, and <strong>cracked lips</strong>. She says her symptoms gradually began <strong>4 months ago</strong> after moving away from home for college. She also complains of <strong>photophobia</strong>, <strong>spontaneous lacrimation</strong>, and <strong>itchy dermatitis</strong>. Past medical history is significant for a <strong>long-standing malabsorption syndrome</strong>, which she says that she <strong>hasn’t been able to maintain her normal diet or take her vitamins regularly</strong> due to her busy schedule. The patient is <strong>afebrile</strong> and <strong>vital signs are within normal limits</strong>. On physical examination, she has a <strong>malnourished appearance</strong> with <strong>significant pallor</strong>. <strong>Conjunctival injection</strong> is present <strong>bilaterally</strong>. Which of the following diagnostic tests will be most helpful to support the diagnosis of the most likely <strong>vitamin deficiency</strong> in this patient?

A. Measurement of erythrocyte glutamic oxaloacetic transaminase activity
B. Measurement of serum methylmalonic acid levels
C. Measurement of erythrocyte folate levels
D. Measurement of erythrocyte glutathione reductase activity

LLM answer: D. Measurement of erythrocyte glutathione reductase activity
