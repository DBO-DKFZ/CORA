A previously healthy <strong>30-year-old woman</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>6-month history</strong> of a recurring <strong>rash</strong> that typically occurs on exposure to the <strong>sun</strong> and affects only the <strong>face</strong>. She also has noticed several <strong>nonpainful ulcers</strong> on the <strong>roof of her mouth</strong>. She is sexually active with one male partner and they use condoms inconsistently. Her mother has <strong>end-stage renal disease</strong>. The patient does not smoke or drink alcohol. Her vital signs are within normal limits. Physical examination shows an <strong>erythematous rash across the cheeks that spares the nasolabial folds</strong>. There are <strong>three small ulcers on the hard palate</strong>. Laboratory studies show:
<strong>Leukocyte count 3,000/mm3</strong>
<strong>Platelet count 70,000/mm3</strong>
<strong>Erythrocyte sedimentation rate 80 mm/h</strong>
Serum
<strong>Antinuclear antibodies 1:320</strong>
<strong>Anti-Smith antibodies positive</strong>
Urine
<strong>Protein 3+</strong>
<strong>RBC casts negative</strong>
<strong>RBCs none</strong>
<strong>WBCs 10–15/hpf</strong>
Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Skin biopsy
B. Renal biopsy
C. Administration of azathioprine
D. Pathergy skin testing

LLM answer: A. Skin biopsy
