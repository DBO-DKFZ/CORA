A <strong>7-year-old boy</strong> is brought to his pediatrician by his parents because of a <strong>new rash</strong>. The family <strong>immigrated from Laos</strong> one year ago and recently obtained <strong>health insurance</strong>. A week ago, the boy stated that he was <strong>“not feeling well”</strong> and asked to stay home from school. At the time, he started having <strong>cough</strong>, <strong>nasal congestion</strong>, and <strong>irritated eyes</strong> – symptoms that persisted and intensified. His parents recall that at the time they noticed <strong>small whitish-blue papules</strong> over the <strong>red buccal mucosa opposite his molars</strong>. <strong>Five days ago</strong>, his parents noticed a <strong>red rash around his face</strong> that quickly <strong>spread downward</strong> to cover most of his <strong>arms, trunk, and then legs</strong>. His temperature is <strong>102.5°F (39.2°C)</strong>, blood pressure is 110/85 mmHg, pulse is 102/min, and respirations 25/min. On physical exam, he has <strong>intermittent cough</strong>, <strong>cervical lymphadenopathy</strong>, and <strong>nonpurulent conjunctivitis</strong> accompanied by a <strong>confluent, dark red rash</strong> over his body. This patient is at risk for which of the following <strong>complications later in life</strong>?

A. CNS degeneration
B. Valvular heart disease
C. Nonreactive pupils
D. Monoarticular arthritis

LLM answer: B. Valvular heart disease
