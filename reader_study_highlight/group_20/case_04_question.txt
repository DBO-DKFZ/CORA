A <strong>63-year-old woman</strong> presents to the <strong>outpatient clinic</strong> complaining of <strong>severe vulvar itching</strong>. The <strong>pruritus</strong> started <strong>1 year ago</strong> and became <strong>worse over the last several months</strong>. She has tried <strong>over-the-counter topical steroids without relief</strong>. She is <strong>not currently sexually active</strong>. Her medical history is notable for <strong>long-standing lichen sclerosus</strong>. The physical examination reveals an <strong>ulcerated small nodule on the right labium majus</strong>, as well as <strong>dry, thin, white lesions encircling the genital and perianal areas</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Bartholin gland cyst
B. Bartholin gland carcinoma
C. Squamous cell carcinoma
D. Vulvar Paget's disease

LLM answer: C. Squamous cell carcinoma
