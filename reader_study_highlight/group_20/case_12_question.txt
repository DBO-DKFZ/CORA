A <strong>57-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician because of <strong>generalized malaise</strong>, <strong>yellowish discoloration of the eyes</strong>, and <strong>pruritus</strong> on the <strong>back of his hands</strong> that <strong>worsens when exposed to sunlight</strong> for the <strong>past several months</strong>. He has <strong>not seen a physician in 15 years</strong>. Physical examination shows <strong>scleral icterus</strong> and <strong>mild jaundice</strong>. There is a <strong>purpuric rash</strong> with <strong>several small vesicles</strong> and <strong>hyperpigmented lesions</strong> on the <strong>dorsum of both hands</strong>. The <strong>causal pathogen</strong> of this patient's <strong>underlying condition</strong> was most likely acquired in which of the following ways?

A. Bathing in freshwater
B. Ingestion of raw shellfish
C. Needlestick injury
D. Inhalation of spores
E. Sexual contact

LLM answer: C. Needlestick injury
