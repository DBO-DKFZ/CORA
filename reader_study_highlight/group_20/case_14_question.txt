A <strong>25-year-old woman</strong> comes to the physician because she has noted darkening of the skin around her neck since wearing a chain she recently bought at a thrift shop. The darkening occurred gradually over the past <strong>2 months</strong> and is accompanied by <strong>thickening</strong> of the affected skin. She has <strong>peptic ulcer disease</strong>. <strong>Menses occur at irregular 35- to 60-day intervals</strong> and last for <strong>9 days with heavy flow</strong>. <strong>Menarche was at the age of 14 years</strong> and her <strong>last menstrual period was 3 weeks ago</strong>. She is <strong>sexually active</strong> with her husband and they <strong>do not use contraception</strong>. The patient's only medication is <strong>cimetidine</strong>. She is <strong>163 cm (5 ft 4 in)</strong> tall and weighs <strong>91 kg (200 lb)</strong>; <strong>BMI is 34 kg/m2</strong>. <strong>Vital signs are within normal limits</strong>. Physical examination shows <strong>velvety, hyperpigmented plaques</strong> in the <strong>axillae</strong>, the <strong>inframammary fold</strong>, and around the <strong>neck</strong>. The remainder of the examination shows no abnormalities. Further evaluation of this patient is most likely to show which of the following findings?

A. Diffusely enlarged thyroid gland on ultrasonography of the neck
B. Elevated serum 17-hydroxyprogesterone levels
C. Malignant glandular cells on gastric biopsy
D. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasonography of the pelvis

LLM answer: D. Polycystic ovaries on ultrasonography of the pelvis
