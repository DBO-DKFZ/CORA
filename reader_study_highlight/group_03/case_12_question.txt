A <strong>52-year-old man</strong> presents to the office for evaluation of a <strong>‘weird rash’</strong> that appeared over his <strong>torso</strong> <strong>last week</strong>. The patient states that the rash just seemed to appear, but denies <strong>itching</strong>, <strong>pain</strong>, or <strong>exposure</strong>. On physical examination, the patient has <strong>multiple light brown-colored flat plaques</strong> on the <strong>torso</strong>. They appear to be <strong>‘stuck on’</strong> but do not have associated <strong>erythema</strong> or <strong>swelling</strong>. What is the most likely indication of the patient’s clinical presentation?

A. Basal cell carcinoma (BCC)
B. Infection with a Poxvirus
C. Gastric adenocarcinoma
D. Slow-growing squamous cell carcinoma

LLM answer: B. Infection with a Poxvirus
