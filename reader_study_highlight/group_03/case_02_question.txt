A <strong>52-year-old woman</strong> with <strong>type 2 diabetes mellitus</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>2-day history</strong> of <strong>blisters on her forearms</strong> and <strong>pain during sexual intercourse</strong>. Her only medications are <strong>metformin</strong> and <strong>glyburide</strong>. Examination reveals <strong>multiple, flaccid blisters</strong> on the <strong>volar surface of the forearms</strong> and <strong>ulcers on the buccal, gingival, and vulvar mucosa</strong>. The <strong>epidermis</strong> on the forearm <strong>separates when the skin is lightly stroked</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome
B. Pemphigus vulgaris
C. Behcet disease
D. Lichen planus
E. Dermatitis herpetiformis
F. Toxic epidermal necrolysis
G. Bullous pemphigoid

LLM answer: B. Pemphigus vulgaris
