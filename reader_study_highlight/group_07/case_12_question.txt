A <strong>previously healthy 27-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician <strong>4 weeks</strong> after noticing <strong>three nontender lesions on his penis</strong>. He says they have <strong>not changed in size</strong>. He is <strong>sexually active with multiple male and female partners</strong> and uses <strong>condoms inconsistently</strong>. He takes no medications. He drinks two to five beers on social occasions. He occasionally smokes marijuana. His temperature is <strong>36.9°C (98.4°F)</strong>. There is <strong>no lymphadenopathy</strong>. Examination shows <strong>three sessile, flesh-colored lesions on the shaft of the penis</strong> that are <strong>10 mm in diameter</strong>. On application of a <strong>dilute solution of acetic acid</strong>, the lesions <strong>turn white</strong>. The remainder of the examination shows no abnormalities. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Oral acyclovir therapy
B. Oral doxycycline therapy
C. Intramuscular penicillin therapy
D. Cryotherapy

LLM answer: D. Cryotherapy
