A <strong>60-year-old woman</strong> presents to the dermatologist with a lesion on her <strong>lower eyelid</strong>. She noticed it <strong>a month ago</strong> and it looked like a <strong>pimple</strong>. She says that it has been <strong>bleeding lately with minimal trauma</strong> which alarmed her. She says the lesion has <strong>not grown in size</strong> and is <strong>not associated with pain or pruritus</strong>. <strong>No significant past medical history</strong>. Physical examination reveals a <strong>0.5 cm lesion</strong> that has a <strong>pearly appearance</strong> with <strong>telangiectasia</strong> and <strong>central ulceration</strong> and <strong>curled borders</strong>. The lesion is <strong>biopsied</strong>. Histopathology reveals <strong>peripheral palisading cells</strong> with <strong>large, hyperchromatic nuclei</strong> and a <strong>high nuclear: cytoplasmic ratio</strong>. Which of the following mechanisms best describes the most common mode of spread of this patient’s neoplasm?

A. Hematogenous spread
B. Seeding
C. Does not spread (tumor is typically benign)
D. Local invasion via collagenase
E. Lymphatic spread

LLM answer: D. Local invasion via collagenase
