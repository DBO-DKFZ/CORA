A <strong>58-year-old</strong> <strong>white man</strong> with <strong>hypertension</strong> and <strong>type 2 diabetes mellitus</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>3-month history</strong> of a <strong>painless lesion</strong> on his <strong>lower lip</strong>. He has <strong>smoked one pack of cigarettes daily for 20 years</strong>. He has worked as a <strong>fruit picker for the past 25 years</strong>. His current medications include <strong>captopril</strong> and <strong>metformin</strong>. Examination of the <strong>oral cavity</strong> shows a <strong>single ulcer near the vermillion border</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Squamous cell carcinoma
B. Aphthous stomatitis
C. Actinic keratosis
D. Traumatic ulcer

LLM answer: A. Squamous cell carcinoma
