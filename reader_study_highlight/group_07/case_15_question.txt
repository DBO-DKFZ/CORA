A <strong>5-month-old male infant</strong> is brought to the physician by his mother because of a <strong>generalized pruritic rash</strong> for <strong>2 weeks</strong>. The <strong>itchiness</strong> often causes the infant to <strong>wake up at night</strong>. He was <strong>strictly breastfed until 4 months of age</strong>, when he was <strong>transitioned to formula feeding</strong>. His father has a history of <strong>asthma</strong>. His <strong>immunizations are up-to-date</strong>. He is at the <strong>75th percentile for length</strong> and the <strong>70th percentile for weight</strong>. Examination shows <strong>dry and scaly patches</strong> on the <strong>face</strong> and <strong>extensor surfaces of the extremities</strong>. The <strong>groin is spared</strong>. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?

A. Topical coal tar
B. Oral acyclovir
C. Oral vitamin A
D. Topical emollient

LLM answer: D. Topical emollient
