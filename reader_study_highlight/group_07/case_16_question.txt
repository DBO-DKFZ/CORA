A <strong>48-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>skin lesion on his nose</strong> and <strong>in his mouth</strong>. The lesions have been <strong>gradually increasing in size</strong> and are <strong>not painful or pruritic</strong>. <strong>Two months ago</strong>, he was treated for <strong>esophageal candidiasis</strong>. Physical examination shows one <strong>pinkish-brown papule</strong> on the <strong>right wing of the nose</strong> and two similar <strong>nodular lesions</strong> on the <strong>hard palate</strong> and <strong>buccal mucosa</strong>. A <strong>biopsy</strong> of one of the lesions shows <strong>spindle-shaped endothelial cells</strong> and infiltration of <strong>lymphocytes, plasma cells, and macrophages</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely causal organism of this patient's condition?

A. Polyomavirus
B. Coccidioides immitis
C. Poxvirus
D. Bartonella henselae
E. Epstein-Barr virus
F. Human herpes virus 8
G. Human T-cell leukemia virus type 1
H. Human papilloma virus 16
I. Human papilloma virus 6
J. Mycobacterium avium complex

LLM answer: D. Bartonella henselae
