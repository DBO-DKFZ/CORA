A <strong>50-year-old man</strong> presents because of a <strong>pruritic rash</strong> of <strong>several years' duration</strong>. The rash is characterized by <strong>erythematous</strong>, <strong>eczematoid patches</strong>, and <strong>raised plaques</strong> and is distributed <strong>asymmetrically</strong> over the <strong>chest</strong> and <strong>abdomen</strong>. <strong>Biopsy</strong> of the plaques reveals <strong>atypical CD4+ T cells</strong> with <strong>cerebriform nuclei</strong>. Further marker studies lead to a diagnosis of <strong>mycosis fungoides</strong>. Which of the following is true of this disease?

A. The disease eventually disseminates to lymph nodes and internal organs.
B. The neoplastic cells most commonly display cell markers of CD19 and CD20.
C. The skin rash most commonly disappears over time.
D. This disease is caused by a chronic fungal infection in the skin.

LLM answer: A. The disease eventually disseminates to lymph nodes and internal organs.
