A <strong>19-year-old woman</strong> presents to the <strong>dermatology clinic</strong> for a <strong>follow-up</strong> of <strong>worsening acne</strong>. She has previously tried <strong>topical tretinoin</strong> as well as <strong>topical and oral antibiotics</strong> with <strong>no improvement</strong>. She recently moved to the area for college and says the acne has caused <strong>significant emotional distress</strong> when it comes to making new friends. She has <strong>no significant past medical or surgical history</strong>. <strong>Family and social history</strong> are also <strong>noncontributory</strong>. The patient’s <strong>blood pressure is 118/77 mm Hg</strong>, the <strong>pulse is 76/min</strong>, the <strong>respiratory rate is 17/min</strong>, and the <strong>temperature is 36.6°C (97.9°F)</strong>. Physical examination reveals <strong>erythematous skin lesions</strong> including both <strong>open and closed comedones</strong> with <strong>inflammatory lesions</strong> overlying her <strong>face, neck, and upper back</strong>. The patient asks about <strong>oral isotretinoin</strong>. Which of the following is the <strong>most important step in counseling</strong> this patient prior to <strong>prescribing oral isotretinoin</strong>?

A. Wear a wide-brimmed hat outdoors
B. Use non-comedogenic sunscreen daily with SPF of at least 45
C. Document 2 negative urine or blood pregnancy tests before beginning oral isotretinoin
D. Apply topical retinoids in the evening before bed

LLM answer: C. Document 2 negative urine or blood pregnancy tests before beginning oral isotretinoin
