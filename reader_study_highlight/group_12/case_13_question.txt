A <strong>62-year-old woman</strong> presents to the office because she has noticed <strong>yellowish bumps and patches</strong> on her <strong>elbows and knees</strong> that seem to come and go. Recently she noticed the same <strong>yellow bumps on her eyelids</strong>. She is a new patient and reports that she is otherwise healthy but did not have insurance until recently so she has not been to the doctor in <strong>over 8 years</strong>. Past medical history is significant for <strong>occasional headaches</strong> that she treats with <strong>aspirin</strong>. She used to <strong>smoke a pack a day for the last 20 years</strong> but recently quit. Her <strong>father died of a heart attack at the age of 55 years</strong> and her <strong>mother had a stroke at 64</strong> and lives in a nursing home. Her <strong>blood pressure is 135/87 mm Hg</strong>, the <strong>heart rate is 95/min</strong>, the <strong>respiratory rate is 12/min</strong>, and the <strong>temperature is 37.0°C (98.6°F)</strong>. On physical exam, she has multiple <strong>tan-yellow, firm papules</strong> on her <strong>knees and elbows</strong>. The papules around her eyes are <strong>smaller and soft</strong>. You discuss the <strong>likely cause of the bumps</strong> and explain that you will need to <strong>order additional tests</strong>. What <strong>test should you perform</strong>?

A. Biopsy
B. PET scan
C. Celiac panel
D. Lipid panel
E. Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)

LLM answer: D. Lipid panel
