A <strong>4-day-old newborn</strong> is brought to the physician because of a <strong>generalized rash</strong> for <strong>1 day</strong>. He was <strong>born at term</strong>. The mother had <strong>no prenatal care</strong> and has a history of <strong>gonorrhea</strong>, which was <strong>treated 4 years ago</strong>. The newborn is at the <strong>50th percentile for head circumference</strong>, <strong>60th percentile for length</strong>, and <strong>55th percentile for weight</strong>. His temperature is <strong>36.8°C (98.2°F)</strong>, pulse is <strong>152/min</strong>, and respirations are <strong>51/min</strong>. Examination shows an <strong>erythematous maculopapular rash</strong> and <strong>pustules with an erythematous base</strong> over the <strong>trunk and extremities</strong>, <strong>sparing the palms and soles</strong>. The remainder of the examination shows <strong>no abnormalities</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Congenital candidiasis
B. Staphylococcal skin scalded syndrome
C. Acropustulosis
D. Erythema toxicum
E. Milia
F. Pustular melanosis
G. Cutis marmorata
H. Congenital syphilis

LLM answer: D. Erythema toxicum
