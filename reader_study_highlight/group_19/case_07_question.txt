A <strong>45-year-old man</strong> presents with a <strong>long history of ulcers on the bottom of his feet</strong>. He recalls having a <strong>similar looking ulcer on the side of his penis</strong> when he was <strong>19 years old</strong> for which he <strong>never sought treatment</strong>. The patient denies any <strong>fever</strong>, <strong>chills</strong>, or <strong>constitutional symptoms</strong>. He reports <strong>multiple sexual partners</strong> and a very <strong>promiscuous sexual history</strong>. He has also <strong>traveled extensively</strong> as a writer since he was 19. The patient is <strong>afebrile</strong>, and his <strong>vital signs are within normal limits</strong>. A <strong>rapid plasma reagin (RPR) test is positive</strong>, and the result of a <strong>Treponema pallidum particle agglutination (TP-PA)</strong> is pending. Which of the following findings would most likely be present in this patient?

A. Hyperreflexia
B. Wide-based gait with a low step
C. Positive Romberg's sign
D. Memory loss
E. Agraphesthesia

LLM answer: C. Positive Romberg's sign
