A <strong>70-year-old woman</strong> comes to the physician for the evaluation of <strong>back pain</strong>. For the past <strong>six days</strong>, she has had a <strong>burning pain</strong> in her <strong>upper right back and chest</strong>. She cannot recall any recent injury and has no prior history of back pain. She has a history of <strong>hypertension</strong> and <strong>gastroesophageal reflux</strong>. The patient volunteers at an animal shelter three times a week. She does not smoke or drink alcohol. Current medications include <strong>ramipril</strong> and <strong>pantoprazole</strong>. The patient appears healthy and well nourished. Her temperature is <strong>36.9°C (98.42°F)</strong>, pulse is <strong>76/min</strong>, and blood pressure is <strong>145/92 mm Hg</strong>. Examination shows a <strong>long erythematous rash</strong> covered with <strong>multiple clear vesicles and crusty lesions</strong> extending from her back to below her <strong>right breast</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely underlying mechanism of the disease?

A. Viral reactivation in dorsal root ganglia
B. IgA deposits in the upper dermis
C. Bacterial lymphatic infection of the epidermis
D. Infestation with bloodsucking parasites

LLM answer: A. Viral reactivation in dorsal root ganglia
