An <strong>11-year-old boy</strong> is brought to the physician for a <strong>follow-up examination</strong>. He has been having <strong>difficulties with his schoolwork</strong> over the past <strong>4 years</strong>. He has a <strong>seizure disorder</strong> treated with <strong>valproic acid</strong>. He was able to <strong>walk independently at the age of 3 years</strong> and was able to <strong>use a fork and spoon at the age of 4 years</strong>. He is at the <strong>40th percentile for height</strong> and <strong>60th percentile for weight</strong>. <strong>Vital signs are within normal limits</strong>. Examination shows <strong>multiple freckles in the axillary and groin skin folds</strong> as well as <strong>scoliosis</strong>. There are <strong>14 hyperpigmented macules</strong> over the <strong>back and chest</strong>. There are <strong>multiple soft, painless nodules</strong> over the <strong>extremities</strong> and the <strong>trunk</strong>. <strong>Ophthalmic examination</strong> shows <strong>hyperpigmented iris nodules bilaterally</strong>. This patient is at increased risk for which of the following conditions?

A. Optic glioma
B. Vestibular schwannoma
C. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma
D. Glaucoma
E. Renal cell carcinoma
F. Giant cell astrocytoma

LLM answer: A. Optic glioma
