A <strong>6-month-old male infant</strong> is brought to a <strong>pediatrician</strong> by his guardian for <strong>scheduled immunizations</strong>. The boy was born at <strong>39 weeks gestation</strong> via <strong>spontaneous vaginal delivery</strong>. He is <strong>up to date on all vaccines</strong> and is <strong>meeting all developmental milestones</strong>. The infant is generally healthy; however, the guardian is concerned about <strong>multiple patches of bluish discolorations</strong> on the skin overlying the <strong>lower back and sacrum</strong>. A review of medical records indicates that these patches have been <strong>present since birth</strong>. On further review the child was placed into <strong>protective services</strong> due to <strong>neglect and abuse</strong> by his biological family. On physical examination, his <strong>vital signs are normal</strong>. The pediatrician notes the presence of <strong>multiple blue-brown patches</strong> over the <strong>lumbosacral region, buttocks, and back</strong>. These patches are <strong>soft and nontender</strong> on palpation. Which of the following is the <strong>best next step in management</strong> of the infant?

A. Reassurance
B. Topical hydrocortisone cream
C. Skin biopsy
D. Inform child protective services
E. Radiographic skeletal survey

LLM answer: D. Inform child protective services
