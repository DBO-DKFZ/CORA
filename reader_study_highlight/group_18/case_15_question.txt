A <strong>33-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician for evaluation of <strong>progressive hair loss</strong> from his <strong>scalp</strong>. He first noticed <strong>receding of the hairline</strong> over the <strong>bitemporal regions</strong> of his scalp <strong>5 years ago</strong>. Since then, his hair has gradually become thinner over the <strong>crown of his head</strong>. He is otherwise healthy and takes no medications. Examination shows <strong>diffuse, nonscarring hair loss</strong> over the scalp with a <strong>bitemporal pattern of recession</strong>. Administration of which of the following drugs is most appropriate to treat this patient's <strong>hair loss</strong>?

A. Clomipramine
B. Triamcinolone
C. Levothyroxine
D. Finasteride

LLM answer: D. Finasteride
