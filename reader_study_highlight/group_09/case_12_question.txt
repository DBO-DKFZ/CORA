A <strong>65-year-old</strong> man who is <strong>quadriplegic</strong> as a result of <strong>multiple sclerosis</strong> is hospitalized for treatment of <strong>left lower lobe pneumonia</strong>. His <strong>temperature</strong> is <strong>38.1°C (100.5°F)</strong>, <strong>pulse</strong> is <strong>95/min</strong>, <strong>respirations</strong> are <strong>12/min</strong>, and <strong>blood pressure</strong> is <strong>120/80 mm Hg</strong>. He appears <strong>malnourished</strong>. <strong>Rhonchi</strong> are heard at the <strong>left lower lobe</strong> of the lung on auscultation. Examination of the heart, lymph nodes, abdomen, and extremities shows no abnormalities. There is a <strong>1-cm area of erythema over the sacrum</strong> with <strong>intact skin</strong> and <strong>no induration</strong>. Neurologic examination shows <strong>quadriparesis</strong>. Test of the stool for occult blood is negative. Which of the following is the most effective intervention for this patient's <strong>skin lesion</strong>?

A. Frequent turning
B. Use of wet to dry dressings
C. Whirlpool therapy
D. Broad-spectrum antibiotic therapy

LLM answer: A. Frequent turning
