A <strong>4-year-old boy</strong> is brought to the <strong>emergency department</strong> by his mother with an <strong>itchy rash</strong> on his <strong>trunk</strong>, <strong>malaise</strong>, and <strong>fever with spikes up to 38.5°C (101.3°F) for the past 2 weeks</strong>. The patient’s mother says she tried giving him <strong>Tylenol</strong> with <strong>little improvement</strong>. Past medical history includes a <strong>spontaneous vaginal delivery at full term</strong>. The patient’s <strong>vaccines are up-to-date</strong> and he has met all <strong>developmental milestones</strong>. On physical examination, his <strong>lips are cracking</strong>, and he has <strong>painful cervical lymphadenopathy</strong>. The rash is <strong>morbilliform</strong> and involves his <strong>trunk, palms, and the soles of his feet</strong>. There is <strong>fine desquamation of the skin of the perianal region</strong>. Which of the following <strong>anatomical structures</strong> is most important to screen for possible complications in this patient?

A. Gallbladder
B. Mitral valve
C. Coronary artery
D. Kidneys
E. Pylorus

LLM answer: C. Coronary artery
