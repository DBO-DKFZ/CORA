A <strong>58-year-old</strong> <strong>white man</strong> with <strong>hypertension</strong> and <strong>type 2 diabetes mellitus</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>3-month history</strong> of a <strong>painless lesion</strong> on his <strong>lower lip</strong>. He has <strong>smoked one pack of cigarettes daily for 20 years</strong>. He has worked as a <strong>fruit picker</strong> for the past <strong>25 years</strong>. His current medications include <strong>captopril</strong> and <strong>metformin</strong>. Examination of the oral cavity shows a <strong>single ulcer near the vermillion border</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Squamous cell carcinoma
B. Herpes labialis
C. Pemphigus vulgaris
D. Aphthous stomatitis
E. Actinic keratosis
F. Traumatic ulcer
G. Basal cell carcinoma

LLM answer: A. Squamous cell carcinoma
