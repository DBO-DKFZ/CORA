A <strong>42-year-old woman</strong> with <strong>well-controlled HIV</strong> on <strong>antiretroviral therapy</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>2-week history</strong> of a <strong>painless lesion</strong> on her <strong>right calf</strong>. Many years ago, she had a <strong>maculopapular rash</strong> over her <strong>trunk, palms, and soles</strong> that <strong>resolved spontaneously</strong>. Physical examination shows a <strong>4-cm firm, non-tender, indurated ulcer</strong> with a <strong>moist, dark base</strong> and <strong>rolled edges</strong>. There is a <strong>similar lesion at the anus</strong>. Results of <strong>rapid plasma reagin testing are positive</strong>. Which of the following findings is most likely on microscopic examination of these lesions?

A. Epithelioid cell infiltrate surrounding acellular, granular core
B. Epidermal hyperplasia with dermal lymphocytic infiltrate
C. Lichenoid hyperplasia with superficial neutrophilic infiltrate
D. Ulcerated epidermis with plasma cell infiltrate
E. Coagulative necrosis surrounded by fibroblast and macrophage infiltrate

LLM answer: D. Ulcerated epidermis with plasma cell infiltrate
