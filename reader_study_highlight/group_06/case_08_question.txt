A <strong>sexually active</strong> <strong>37-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician because of a <strong>7-day history</strong> of <strong>itching</strong> in the area of his <strong>genitals</strong>. He also reports <strong>burning on micturition</strong>. He has <strong>type 2 diabetes mellitus</strong>, which is <strong>well controlled</strong> with <strong>oral metformin</strong>. <strong>Pelvic examination</strong> shows <strong>tender</strong>, <strong>atrophic white papules</strong> on the <strong>glans</strong> and <strong>prepuce</strong>, with <strong>erythema</strong> of the surrounding skin. The <strong>urinary meatus</strong> is <strong>narrowed</strong> and <strong>sclerotic</strong>. Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in <strong>diagnosis</strong>?

A. Test lesion with acetic acid application
B. Local application of clobetasol
C. Local application of fluconazole
D. Biopsy of the lesion
E. KOH examination of skin scrapings
F. Local application of tacrolimus

LLM answer: D. Biopsy of the lesion
