A <strong>70-year-old man</strong> is at his dermatologist’s office for the treatment of a <strong>severely pruritic erythroderma</strong> with <strong>scaling on his buttocks</strong> that has been <strong>slowly progressing over the past two weeks</strong>. The patient works as a <strong>truck driver</strong> and has a history of <strong>hypertension treated with enalapril</strong>. The patient reports having tried an <strong>over-the-counter cream</strong> on the rash without improvement. The <strong>vital signs are within normal range</strong>. On physical exam, he has <strong>multiple confluent and well-demarcated pink patches</strong> on his <strong>buttocks and legs</strong> with some <strong>scaling</strong> and <strong>enlarged inguinal lymph nodes</strong>. The dermatologist orders a <strong>skin biopsy</strong> that reveals <strong>Pautrier microabscesses</strong>. What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Psoriasis
B. Lichen planus
C. Atopic dermatitis
D. Mycosis fungoides

LLM answer: D. Mycosis fungoides
