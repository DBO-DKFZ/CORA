A <strong>16-year-old boy</strong> is brought to the physician by his <strong>host parents</strong> for evaluation of a <strong>progressively pruritic rash</strong> over his <strong>shoulders and buttocks</strong> for the past <strong>6 months</strong>. He recently came to the <strong>United States from Nigeria</strong> to attend a year of high school. He reports that it has been increasingly difficult for him to <strong>read the whiteboard</strong> during classes. Physical examination shows <strong>symmetrically distributed papules 4–8 mm in diameter</strong>, <strong>excoriation marks</strong>, and <strong>patchy hyperpigmentation</strong> over his <strong>shoulders, waist, and buttocks</strong>. There is <strong>nontender inguinal lymphadenopathy</strong> and several <strong>firm, nontender subcutaneous nodules</strong> along the <strong>right iliac crest</strong>. <strong>Six skin snip biopsies</strong> are taken from the <strong>pelvic girdle, buttocks, and thigh</strong>, and are then <strong>incubated in saline</strong>. After <strong>24 hours</strong>, microscopic examination shows <strong>motile microfilariae</strong>. Which of the following is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Lymphatic filariasis
B. Onchocerciasis
C. Cysticercosis
D. Cutaneous larva migrans

LLM answer: B. Onchocerciasis
