A <strong>3-year-old male child</strong> is found to have a <strong>disease involving DNA repair</strong>. Specifically, he is found to have a <strong>defect in the endonucleases</strong> involved in the <strong>nucleotide excision repair</strong> of <strong>pyrimidine dimers</strong>. Which of the following is a unique late-stage complication of this child's disease?

A. Colorectal cancer
B. Endometrial cancer
C. Lymphomas
D. Telangiectasia
E. Malignant melanoma

LLM answer: E. Malignant melanoma
