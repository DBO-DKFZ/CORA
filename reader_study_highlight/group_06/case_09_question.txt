A <strong>74-year-old man</strong> comes to the physician for evaluation of a <strong>skin lesion</strong> on his <strong>right arm</strong>. The lesion first appeared <strong>3 months ago</strong> and has since been <strong>slowly enlarging</strong>. Physical examination shows a <strong>1.5-centimeter</strong>, <strong>faintly erythematous</strong>, <strong>raised lesion</strong> with <strong>irregular borders</strong> on the <strong>dorsum of the right forearm</strong>. A <strong>biopsy specimen</strong> is obtained. If present, which of the following <strong>histopathological features</strong> would be most consistent with <strong>carcinoma in situ</strong>?

A. Pleomorphism of cells in the stratum corneum
B. Irreversible nuclear changes in the stratum basale
C. Full-thickness basal to apical cell polarity
D. Increased nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio in the stratum spinosum
E. Presence of epithelial cells in the dermis
F. High mitotic activity in the stratum granulosum

LLM answer: D. Increased nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio in the stratum spinosum
