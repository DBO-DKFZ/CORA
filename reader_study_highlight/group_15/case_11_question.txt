A <strong>64-year-old man</strong> arrived at the clinic with a <strong>severely painful rash</strong> and <strong>skin eruptions</strong> that are <strong>localized entirely on one side of his body</strong>, closely following the <strong>dermatome level of spinal nerve C7</strong>. The patient was diagnosed with <strong>herpes zoster virus</strong>. In what structure has the virus most likely proliferated to cause the patient's current condition?

A. The sympathetic chain
B. The dorsal root ganglion of the C7 spinal nerve
C. The lateral horn of the C7 spinal cord segment
D. The posterior cutaneous branch of the dorsal primary ramus of C7

LLM answer: B. The dorsal root ganglion of the C7 spinal nerve
