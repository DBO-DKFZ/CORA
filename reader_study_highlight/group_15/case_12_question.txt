A <strong>24-year-old</strong> <strong>sexually active</strong> man complains of <strong>painless growths</strong> on his <strong>penis</strong>. He is worried that he might have transmitted them to his <strong>girlfriend</strong>. Biopsy shows <strong>squamous cells</strong> with <strong>perinuclear cytoplasmic vacuolization</strong>, <strong>nuclear enlargement</strong>, and <strong>koilocytes</strong>. The doctor treats the patient by chemically ablating the <strong>warts</strong> with <strong>cryoablation</strong>. The patient encourages his girlfriend to get tested too, as he is worried she is at increased risk of developing a <strong>malignancy</strong>. Which <strong>cancer</strong> is the patient worried about?

A. Kaposi sarcoma
B. Burkitt lymphoma
C. Hepatocellular carcinoma
D. Cervical carcinoma

LLM answer: D. Cervical carcinoma
